## Why I Chased 26% Compounding and Discovered the Real Secret to Wealth Creation

My license plate reads **COMLB26**.

Most people see a random combination of letters and numbers. I see an investing philosophy. The **LB** stands for pound, so in my mind COMLB26 reads as a single instruction:

> **Compound at 26%.**

What fascinated me was never the annual return itself. It was the mathematics hiding behind it.

---

## The 1,000x Equation

At 26% annual compounding, capital doubles roughly every three years. Ten doublings — thirty years — produce a thousandfold result, and the progression from there becomes difficult to believe.

| Years | Round Figure | Actual Multiple |
|------:|-------------:|----------------:|
| 3 | 2× | 2.0× |
| 30 | 1,000× | 1,026× |
| 60 | 1,000,000× | 1,052,526× |
| 90 | 1,000,000,000× | 1,079,814,265× |

Think about that for a moment. A single dollar becomes roughly $1,000 after 30 years, $1,000,000 after 60 years, and $1,000,000,000 after 90 years.

The numbers seem absurd. Yet they are simply the arithmetic consequence of uninterrupted compounding — no leverage, no windfall, no single brilliant trade.

> Compounding is one of the few forces in the world that can transform the ordinary into the extraordinary.

---

## First, An Honest Word About 26%

Before going further, the number deserves calibration, because a table like the one above can quietly imply a promise.

Sustaining 26% a year for decades is not a demanding goal. It is a historically rare one. Warren Buffett compounded Berkshire Hathaway at roughly 20% a year for nearly six decades, and that record is considered among the greatest in the history of capital allocation. Very few investors have matched it, and fewer still have exceeded it for long. The tiny handful who have done so operated with unusual temperament, unusual discipline, and often unusual circumstances.

So COMLB26 is not a forecast, and I have never treated it as one. It is a **direction of travel** — a standard aggressive enough to force serious thinking about how returns are actually earned. The value of an ambitious target is not that you hit it. It is that chasing it honestly teaches you what separates a durable process from a lucky one.

---

## The Most Valuable Asset Is Time

Most investors focus on how much money they have. Far fewer appreciate how valuable their remaining time is.

A newborn child possesses something no billionaire can buy more of.

Time.

Imagine a child receiving a modest investment account at birth and leaving it untouched for decades. The amount matters and the rate of return matters, but the decisive advantage is the head start. At high rates of compounding, decades become overwhelmingly powerful, and the investor becomes progressively less dependent on making new contributions later. Time starts doing the heavy lifting.

This is why policies that encourage investing early in life are so interesting to me. Whether the initial account is large or small, the real gift is the runway.

---

## Learning From Peter Lynch

My journey toward understanding 26% compounding started with a simple question:

> How can a rational investor achieve exceptional long-term returns?

One early clue came from Peter Lynch, who encouraged investors to study businesses they already understand. Public markets swing far more dramatically than the underlying value of the businesses they represent — a quality company may fall 30% or 40% even when little has changed in its long-term prospects, and enthusiasm can just as easily push prices well above intrinsic value.

That gap between price and value is the raw material. Volatility creates opportunity for investors willing to think independently.

---

## Capturing Opportunity

Many stocks move through a wide band over a typical year. Suppose a quality business trades across a range of roughly 50% over twelve months. Most investors view that movement as noise to be endured.

What if a patient investor could occasionally capture a portion of that variance through common sense, valuation discipline, and Munger-like mental models? Not by predicting every move, and not through constant trading — but by purchasing outstanding businesses when they become meaningfully undervalued, and then leaving them alone.

Repeated over decades, even modest advantages compound into extraordinary outcomes. That is the mechanism behind every serious long-term record.

---

## The Munger Influence

The more I studied investing, the more I realized that superior returns rarely come from superior spreadsheets. They come from superior judgment.

Charlie Munger spoke about building a latticework of mental models — understanding incentives, psychology, probability, competitive advantage, and human behavior well enough that the models reinforce one another rather than sitting in isolation.

Great investors do not need hundreds of brilliant ideas. They need a few good ideas and the patience to let them work.

---

## The Unexpected Discovery

Ironically, my journey did not end with the pursuit of 26%.

It began there.

Along the way I encountered several multi-baggers — businesses that multiplied 10×, 20×, or more. Those experiences taught me that wealth creation is rarely linear, and that a handful of exceptional investments can dominate long-term results while the majority of positions contribute very little.

Perhaps karma plays a role. Or perhaps disciplined behavior simply increases the probability that opportunity eventually finds you. Either way, the pursuit changes you — it rewires what you look for and, more importantly, what you refuse to do.

---

## Why Buffett Started Early

When people study Warren Buffett, they focus almost entirely on stock selection. A more important lesson may be that he started investing as a child.

His fortune was built not only through good returns, but through decades of uninterrupted compounding. The overwhelming majority of his net worth was accumulated well after his fiftieth birthday — not because his judgment suddenly improved, but because by then the base was large and the runway behind him was long.

Time amplified every intelligent decision he ever made. The earlier the process begins, the more powerful it becomes.

---

## COMLB26 Was Never Really About 26%

Over time, I realized something surprising. COMLB26 was never really about achieving exactly 26%. It was about understanding the relationship between patience, discipline, and time.

The real lesson is not that everyone can achieve a 26% CAGR. The real lesson is this:

> Extraordinary wealth is often the result of ordinary returns sustained for extraordinary periods.

Twenty-six percent simply makes the mathematics impossible to ignore.

---

## Final Thoughts

The goal was never to get rich overnight, and it was never to chase the next hot stock. The goal was always to build a compounding machine — a repeatable process, a rational framework, and a lifetime of learning.

My license plate still reads **COMLB26**. Today it serves as a reminder that the greatest fortunes are not usually created through miracles. They are created through patience, through thoughtful decisions, through long time horizons, and through the extraordinary power of compounding.

> The goal was never overnight success.
>
> The goal was to compound, learn, and thrive for decades.
>
> Everything else is a byproduct.
